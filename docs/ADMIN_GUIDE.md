# Admin guide

For clinic staff. No technical knowledge assumed.

---

## Signing in

Go to **/staff-login** and use the email and password your administrator gave
you.

Your own account, never a shared one. The system records who did what, and that
record protects you as much as the clinic — it can show you did **not** open a
record as easily as that you did.

---

## Your day

### Front desk

**Admin → Front desk** is the day as a board. Patients move left to right:

**Expected** → **Waiting** → **With the doctor** → **Completed**

When someone arrives, find them under Expected and press **Check in**. They move
to Waiting and the board starts counting how long they have been there. The
badge turns amber after 10 minutes and red after 20, which is your cue to say
something to them before they have to ask.

A red **Clinical alert** badge means something the dentist needs to know —
an allergy, a blood thinner. You see that the flag exists; the details are in
the clinical record, which is the dentist's.

### Confirming bookings

Website bookings arrive as **Requested** and are not yet confirmed. The
**Needs confirming** column, and the badge in the sidebar, show how many are
waiting.

Press **Confirm** and the patient automatically gets a WhatsApp message and an
email, and reminders are scheduled for 24 hours and 2 hours before.

Do not leave these sitting. The patient has been told "we will confirm shortly".

### Cancelling

You must give a reason. This is not bureaucracy — the cancellation report is how
the clinic learns whether people cancel because of cost, timing or something
fixable. "Patient unwell" and "could not get time off work" lead to different
changes.

## Attendance

**Admin → Attendance**. Press **Check in** when you arrive, **Check out** when
you leave.

Forgot to check out? Ask a manager to correct it. They must give a reason, and
you can see the correction and the reason on your own record.

The system records check-in and check-out only. It does not track your location,
your screen or what you do during the day.

---

## Patients

**Admin → Patients**. Search by name, phone, email or patient number. Phone
number is usually fastest.

What you see depends on your role:

- **Reception** — contact details, appointments, invoices. Not clinical notes.
- **Dentists and assistants** — the clinical record too.
- **Marketing** — no patient records at all.

This is not a lack of trust. It is the standard way health records are handled:
people see what their job needs. Every time anyone opens a patient record, that
is recorded.

## Enquiries

**Admin → Leads**. Everyone who has contacted the clinic and not yet been dealt
with.

**New** — nobody has spoken to them yet. These are the priority; a same-day call
converts far better than one three days later.

**Follow-up due** — you said you would call back and the date has passed.

When you log a call, the automated WhatsApp follow-ups stop. A patient who has
spoken to a person should not keep getting automated messages.

When you mark an enquiry **Lost**, say why. That is how the clinic decides where
to spend its advertising money.

---

## Content verification

**Admin → Content verification**. This is where the clinic decides what the
website is allowed to claim.

Every factual statement about the practice — years of experience, patients
treated, equipment, qualifications — is listed here. Anything not verified
**does not appear on the website at all**.

To verify something, you must record what evidence you saw. Not "I think it's
right" — the actual document: "MDS certificate seen 12 March 2026",
"AERB licence XYZ, valid to 2028".

This exists because the old website said the practice had both 25 years and 18
years of experience, and had treated both 20,000 and 5,000 patients. A patient
who notices that stops believing anything else on the page.

Two things need more than an invoice:

- **CBCT** requires a current AERB licence for the machine.
- **Each named specialist** requires their Dental Council registration number.

## Messages

**Admin → Messages** shows everything sent to patients: confirmations,
reminders, receipts.

**Queued** means waiting to send — normally seconds. If the number is large and
growing, tell whoever manages the system: it means reminders are not going out,
and you will not notice for weeks except as more people missing appointments.

**Suppressed** means deliberately not sent, usually because the patient opted
out of WhatsApp. That is correct behaviour, not a fault.

## Common questions

**A patient says they never got their confirmation.**
Open their appointment and look at the messages. If it says Sent, check the
number is right. If it says Suppressed, they have opted out of WhatsApp — use
email or ring them.

**A patient wants to change their appointment.**
Open the appointment and use Reschedule. They are notified automatically. Doing
it this way keeps the history, so if they say later that they were moved twice,
you can see it.

**A patient wants a copy of their records.**
They are entitled to it. Ask your administrator — there is a process, and it
includes checking the person asking is the patient.

**Someone asks for their data to be deleted.**
Do not action it yourself. Pass it to your administrator. Some records must be
kept by law, and the patient is entitled to be told exactly what is kept and
why.

**I can see something I do not think I should.**
Tell your administrator immediately. It is far better to report it than to
ignore it.
